EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Core Principles of EV Charging for Indian Owners

  • ✓ AC vs DC Fundamentals: AC charging relies on your car's onboard converter (slow); DC fast charging bypasses the onboard converter and feeds high-voltage electricity directly to the battery.
  • ✓ The Charging Curve: EVs charge at peak speeds between 10% and 80% SOC; after 80%, charging slows down automatically to prevent lithium plating and thermal stress.
  • ✓ Battery Chemistry: LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) batteries can be charged to 100% routinely; NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt) batteries are best kept between 20% and 80% for daily commuting.
SECTION 1 // ELECTRICAL FOUNDATIONS

How EV Charging Works: Volts, Amps & Kilowatt-Hours

At its electrical core, charging an electric car is analogous to filling a fuel tank, but governed by electrochemistry. Power (measured in kilowatts, kW) represents the speed at which energy is delivered, while capacity (measured in kilowatt-hours, kWh) represents the volume of energy stored inside the battery pack.

Electrical power is calculated by multiplying Voltage (V) by Current in Amperes (A). A standard Indian 15A wall socket operates at 230 Volts: 230V × 15A = 3,450 Watts = ~3.4 kW. Plugging a 40 kWh EV battery into this socket will require approximately 12 hours for a full replenish.

SECTION 2 // CHARGING LEVELS & INFRASTRUCTURE MECHANICS

Level 1, Level 2 & Level 3 DC Fast Charging

LEVEL 1 AC (Portable 3.3 kW 15A Socket)

Included free with every EV in India. Requires no electrical modification other than a dedicated, well-grounded 15A socket. Perfect for overnight home charging, adding 120-150 km of range overnight.

LEVEL 2 AC (7.2 kW to 22 kW Wallbox)

Installed on dedicated single-phase (7.2 kW) or three-phase (11-22 kW) lines at homes, offices, and apartment basements. Completely recharges passenger EVs in 5 to 7 hours.

LEVEL 3 DC (30 kW to 240 kW DC Fast Chargers)

High-voltage commercial chargers located on highways and urban charging hubs using CCS2 connectors. Converts AC grid power directly into 400V–800V DC power, charging vehicles from 10% to 80% in 30–45 minutes.

SECTION 3 // BATTERY HEALTH & THERMAL CARE

Maximizing Battery Health & Lifespan in Warm Indian Climates

GOLDEN RULES FOR BATTERY LONGEVITY
  • • Follow the 20-80 Rule for NMC Batteries: Keep daily battery state of charge between 20% and 80% to minimize chemical stress.
  • • Charge LFP Batteries to 100% Weekly: LFP cells (found in Tata Nexon EV, Punch EV, MG ZS EV) benefit from weekly 100% charges for BMS cell balancing.
  • • Avoid Immediate Fast Charging After Hard Driving: Allow the battery coolant loop 10 minutes to stabilize before plugging into a 120 kW fast charger.
